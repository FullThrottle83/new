# SÖM — game vision

**Status:** creative direction, not locked content specification. **Working title:** SÖM. An original atmospheric 2D puzzle adventure. This document describes our game, not a reconstruction of LIMBO or INSIDE.

## One sentence

A former cable-network engineer crosses a fractured valley of suspended settlements, using a physical grappling tool to restore connections that a central safety system deliberately severed.

## Player and world

Edda is a former line worker, physically vulnerable but technically capable. Settlements cling to cliff walls via bridges, cables, lifts, counterweights and old bell towers. A slowly advancing front of fire, ash and hot gas threatens the lower valley. The central city remains suspended while outer settlements were disconnected. Edda helped install the safety system and gradually discovers its consequences.

The opening is quiet: rain, a repair bench and three distant bells from a long-silent tower. During the early escape she finds a mechanical line launcher. This is the game's defining tool, not a late upgrade. It begins as a way to cross gaps and becomes a way to pull, tension, anchor and connect machinery.

## Journey (working outline)

1. **The last signal:** departure from an isolated workshop; first environmental hazards and the approaching glow front.
2. **The line launcher:** discover the grappling tool and learn a real pendulum swing over a chasm.
3. **The hanging houses:** unstable structures, moving loads and the first signs of survivors.
4. **The greenhouses:** wind, suspended glass and airflow-driven puzzles; evidence of the deliberate disconnection.
5. **The bell valley:** physical bell and resonance mechanisms; other settlements answer.
6. **The core:** the city's load-distribution machinery; reconnecting everyone requires lowering the city onto an older foundation rather than merely switching the old system back on.

The ending remains restrained: Edda leaves her irretrievably attached tool behind. In the distance, people meet on a reconnected bridge; a small bell rings. No explanatory monologue.

## Gameplay pillars

- **A physical grapple.** Fire toward authored surfaces, attach, swing, shorten/lengthen a cable, release with momentum, and use the line to move or connect world objects. Not a universal teleport or combat weapon.
- **Learnable cause and effect.** Momentum, tension, mass, counterweights, wind and moving architecture form puzzles. No unearned coordinate-triggered skips.
- **Vulnerability and consequence.** Precise movement, short checkpoints, readable hazards and knowledge-based progression rather than weapons, health bars or upgrade trees.
- **Environmental storytelling.** Sparse interface, no spoken exposition required; people may be wary or helpful, not merely enemies.
- **Contrast.** Quiet traversal and careful observation alternate with authored physical danger.

## Art direction

Graphite-black silhouettes and textured slate-gray structures against cold blue-gray fog. Muted copper distinguishes Edda's equipment and useful industrial surfaces; warm ember light marks the approaching threat. Layered depth, parallax, cinematic framing, legible foreground collision and restrained effects. **Do not recreate Playdead characters, shots or level geometry.**

## Sound direction

Subtle, mysterious, slightly unsettling low-stimulation ambience. Warm analog textures, distant bronze bells, mechanical resonances and soft environmental detail. Three bells recur as a signal motif. No vocals required; no sudden music peaks as default. Audio cues must remain useful for grapple attachment, cable tension and mechanisms. Provide separate music, ambience and effects controls and a reduced-intensity option as the game matures.

## First playable proof

A short vertical slice: workshop -> approach of the glow front -> bell tower -> grapple acquisition -> one demanding but fair chasm crossing. Validate feel and physics **before** designing the full campaign.

## Explicitly out of scope now

39 levels, full story scripting, custom engine, level editor, accounts, leaderboards, backend services, monetization and public release. All are separate decisions.
